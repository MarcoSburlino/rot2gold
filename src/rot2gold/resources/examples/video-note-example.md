# Spacing reviews over days instead of cramming

> [!note] AI-processed note. The Content section is the creator's words. Everything after it is AI-written and not verified fact unless a source says so.

## Key points

- The creator argues that reviewing material a little each day keeps it longer than one long session the night before.
- The post cites Hermann Ebbinghaus's "forgetting curve" as the reason: memories fade fast unless they are reviewed.
- It suggests a simple schedule: review after one day, three days and a week.
- The creator presents this as their own study habit, not as a study they ran.

If you cram the night before an exam, you will forget most of it by next week. That's not you being bad at studying, that's how memory works.

In the 1880s a guy called Ebbinghaus[?] tested himself on nonsense syllables and found that you lose most of what you learn within a day or two, unless you review it.

So here's what I do. I learn something, I review it the next day, then three days later, then a week later. Each review takes like five minutes, and it sticks way longer than one big session.

## Context and other perspectives

The post describes the spacing effect, usually paired with "retrieval practice" (testing yourself rather than rereading). Research on both is broad, though the best gaps between reviews depend on how long the material needs to be remembered. Some educators point out that cramming can still work for a test the next morning; its weakness is long-term retention, which is what the creator talks about.

Further reading:
- *Make It Stick* by Peter C. Brown, Henry L. Roediger III and Mark A. McDaniel
